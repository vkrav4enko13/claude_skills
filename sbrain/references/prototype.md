# Throwaway prototype

Read when a decision is expensive to redo, conversation and a sketch cannot close it, and the user agreed to a prototype. The technique is taken from compound-engineering (`ce-prototype`, MIT) and set out in our own words.

One named question about how it feels; the user's perception decides.

1. **Name the question and what we count as an answer** — one phrase each, agreed before you start.
2. **Do not fake what is being checked.** Behaviour (how a scenario goes, how an element responds) needs working interaction, not a screen picture. Look (mock-up, colour, density) needs real quality, not a rough sketch.
3. **Build outside the product** — a separate throwaway folder; name its path. Inside the project only for an existing screen or on request: never commit it, clean up after — restore your files, name those you could not.
4. **The user has looked and chosen** — record the choice as a decision, in their words. Do not refine it or carry it into the product; delete only on request.
5. **The answer changed what we build,** rather than closed the question — back to the survey with what came to light.

No person at the screen — no prototype. A question a cheap sketch closes goes by `references/visual.md`.
