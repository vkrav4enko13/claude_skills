# Verdict on a ready-made solution

Read when the question is whether to take a named ready-made thing: "should we move to X?", "X or Y for this project?". Not a brainstorm. The technique is taken from compound-engineering (`ce-pov`, MIT) and set out in our own words.

1. **A verdict is earned by the facts of the project.** Find out yourself: what the project uses now, what is needed from the candidate, which constraints rule it out. Conversation claims are not facts until checked.
2. **Check the candidate against fresh sources:** documentation, limitations, maturity, cost of the move. Name the source.
3. **Do not survey the user to understand what to evaluate.** Missing information that would change the verdict and cannot be found — say what is missing and what would close it.
4. **The answer starts with the decision:** "yes", "no" or "not now". Then what it stands on, what we pay, what is unknown, what would change the verdict.

The verdict is the result: implement nothing. A decision to move is a new task with its own path.
