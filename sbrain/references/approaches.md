# Approaches. Design by parts (the "Large" path)

Read at step 5 — when the survey is finished — and at step 6, when the approach is chosen.

## 5. Approaches

Several plausible directions remain — offer 2–3. **First show all of them, then recommend.** Each: essence in 2–3 sentences, pros and cons, main risk, when it fits, and whether it repeats, extends or is new.

- At least one is non-obvious: the opposite, a removed constraint, a solution from another field.
- Where it fits, one "with benefit to spare" (an addition or reframing raising the benefit at a fair price) — a contender, not the default.
- An approach that fits any project of this kind — sharpen it for this one or drop it.
- Distinguish by mechanism and by what the user gets ("a pause is a property of the rule" vs "a pause is a separate entity"), not by table and file names.
- For a new product approaches differ in **what** we build (product shape, audience, difference from neighbours), not how.

Recommend the one reaching the goal with the smallest addition — the simplest thing with real benefit; the price is what must be maintained, not the effort of writing. One direction — name it plainly; do not invent alternatives.

## 6. Design by parts (the "Large" path)

Show the design in sections in one message; one request, a question per section; a corrected section is reshown and asked alone. Sections: what the user gets, parts, data flow, errors, how we check — a couple of sentences to 200–300 words.

Each part: one job, clear boundaries — what it does, how to use it, what it depends on. Follow existing structure; touch neighbouring code only as the goal needs.

The approved sections go into the topic's `design.md` card as approved.

The "show, not describe" rule of step 4 applies here too.
